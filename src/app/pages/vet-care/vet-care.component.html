Veterinary Care Resources

We know how stressful it is when your pet is sick or hurt, especially when cost is a worry. The SPCA of Tompkins County is an animal shelter, not a veterinary clinic. We can't diagnose or treat illnesses, handle emergencies, or provide ongoing care for owned pets. We do want to help you find the right care, and in some specific cases we may be able to help directly.

Is this an emergency?

Go to an emergency hospital right away if your pet has any of these signs. If you can, call on the way so they can get ready for you.

  • Trouble breathing
  • Pale or blue gums
  • Collapse, or sudden severe weakness or confusion
  • Bleeding that won't stop, or a major injury
  • A swollen, hard belly, or trying to vomit with nothing coming up
  • Straining to urinate with little or nothing coming out
  • Seizures
  • Possible poisoning

Cornell University Hospital for Animals is open 24/7 at (607) 253-3060, 930 Campus Rd, Ithaca. For problems that need care soon but aren't life-threatening, Cornell also offers urgent care.

Where to start: a veterinarian

For illness, injuries, check-ups, and ongoing care, your first stop is a veterinarian. If you don't have one, or if cost is a barrier, these lists can help:

  • Emergency & Veterinary Care Directory: emergency hospitals, local veterinarians by area, and house call vets
  • Low-Cost & Community Resources: free and low-cost clinics, help getting your pet to appointments, and support services for people
  • Financial Assistance for Vet Bills: payment plans, crowdfunding, and grants for emergencies, ongoing illness, and specific conditions

How the SPCA may be able to help

If your pet has already been diagnosed by a veterinarian and needs a one-time surgery, we may be able to help. Examples include:

  • Amputations, including for fractured limbs
  • Eye removal
  • Some mass removals
  • Surgery for a uterine infection (pyometra)
  • Help with a difficult birth (this results in a spay; we don't perform C-sections)
  • Bladder stone surgery
  • Some treatment for urinary blockages in male cats

We can also sometimes help with:

  • Outpatient treatment for parvovirus (dogs) or panleukopenia (cats), depending on how sick the animal is and how much space we have
  • Preventive care clinics (vaccines, deworming, flea and tick), when one is scheduled
  • Spay and neuter, TNR for community cats, and free rabies clinics
  • End-of-life exams for euthanasia services

There is a cost for these services. It's set case by case, depending on your pet's needs. We review every request individually and can't guarantee we'll be able to help.

Help us help you

If your pet has a diagnosis and you think we may be able to help, email info@spcaonline.com with:

  • Your name and phone number
  • Your pet's name, species, and age
  • The diagnosis and the name of the vet who made it
  • Any vet records or estimates you have

The more information you send, the faster we can let you know whether we can help.
