Thinking About Surrendering Your Pet? Let's Talk First.

Deciding whether to give up a pet is hard, and life changes can make it feel like there's no other choice. Our goal is to help keep pets in their homes whenever possible. A shelter is stressful for animals, and many of the problems that lead to surrender can be solved.

Because our space is limited, we save surrender appointments for the animals who need us most. Before contacting us about surrender, please look at the options below.

Is it a medical issue?

Many health problems, from allergies to chronic conditions, can be managed more easily and cheaply than people expect. Talk with your veterinarian first. If cost is a concern, visit our Veterinary Care page for low-cost and assistance options.

Is it a behavior issue?

Problems like house soiling, scratching, barking, or not getting along with other pets often have a medical cause or can be improved with training. Start with your veterinarian, who can rule out health problems and refer you to a trainer or behavior specialist.

Can you rehome your pet yourself?

If you can no longer keep your pet, rehoming them directly is often the kindest choice. Your pet goes straight from your home to a new one and skips the shelter. You also stay in charge of the process: you meet adopters, choose the right fit for your pet, and share everything you know about their personality and needs.

We recommend Rehome by Adopt-a-Pet, a free site where you can create a profile for your pet, review applications, and screen adopters from home. Sharing your pet's listing with friends, family, coworkers, and social media can also help them find a home faster.

Have you tried everything?

If you live in Tompkins County and have used up these resources, we're here to help. We only accept owner surrenders from Tompkins County residents, and only by appointment.

How to request a surrender appointment:

  1. Complete a surrender profile for each pet (forms are linked below, or pick one up at any of our locations).
  2. Send your pet's veterinary records along with the completed form to intake@spcaonline.com.
  3. We'll contact you with a pathway plan. Our intake team will review your pet's information and follow up about next steps, which may include resources to help keep your pet or scheduling a surrender appointment.

Questions? Call (607) 257-1822 x237 or email intake@spcaonline.com.

Surrender Fee

There is a $40 surrender fee per pet. The SPCA of Tompkins County is a private, nonprofit shelter, and we rely on fees and donations to care for the animals in our shelter. If you can't afford the fee, please talk with us before your appointment.
